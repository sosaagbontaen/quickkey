Shipping QuickKey

How a Premiere plugin reaches the editors who want it — and the one thing blocking us from starting.

Status: working prototype Not yet installable by a stranger Updated 5 Sep 2026
Start Here

No distribution question matters until this is fixed

QuickKey cannot currently be installed by someone who doesn’t trust us.

Installing means clicking past a macOS warning that calls the app unverified, and it switches on PlayerDebugMode so Premiere will accept an unsigned extension. Dom got through that because he knows us. A stranger who paid for it will ask for a refund — or post that our plugin told them to weaken a security setting.

Ship a signed ZXP

ZXP is Adobe’s extension package format. A properly signed one installs through normal tooling and needs no PlayerDebugMode at all. This is the highest-value engineering task left.

Signing certificate · ~1 day

Notarise the daemon with Apple

An Apple Developer account lets us sign and notarise the hotkey app, so macOS stops describing it as possible malware on first launch.

Apple Developer Program · ~$99/year · ~1 day

Call it two days and about a hundred dollars. Until it’s done, everything below is theoretical.

Enrol in the Apple Developer Program Enrolment is not instant — identity verification can take days. Start it before it is on the critical path.Do now
Obtain a code-signing certificate for the ZXP A self-signed certificate may be sufficient for normal installation. If so, this costs nothing.Verify
Check whether the signed ZXP removes the debug-mode switch If it does, the ugliest part of installation disappears entirely.Verify
Getting Testers

There is no TestFlight for Adobe plugins

Nothing equivalent exists. No staged rollout, no beta channel, no review queue standing between us and a tester. That sounds like a gap and is mostly a relief: a signed ZXP is just a file we send people. Email it, link it, hand it over. They install it and it works.

Which means the beta programme is not a piece of infrastructure to build. It is a list of names, a download link and a way to hear back.

On the permissions problem

This is the same blocker as above, and it resolves in two halves:

  • The Premiere half disappears with a signed ZXP. No PlayerDebugMode, no security setting to switch on, no instructions that read like a jailbreak.
  • The macOS half disappears with Apple notarisation. The hotkey helper stops being described as unverified software.

What Dom and I had to do — click past warnings, flip a hidden preference — is not a smaller version of what a customer will do. It is a different thing entirely, and no customer will do it. Fix both halves and installation becomes double-click, done.

Channels

How editors actually find plugins

Four routes, and they are emphatically not equal.

1 aescripts + aeplugins The trusted marketplace for After Effects and Premiere tools, and where working editors go looking. It handles payment, licensing and updates for a cut of revenue. Being listed is itself the credibility — many well-known tools in this category launched there rather than selling direct. Do this one
2 YouTube plugin reviewers The discovery engine for editors, and already in our original notes. A tool that turns seven steps into one keystroke is built for a ninety-second segment. High leverage
3 r/editors and r/premiere Where the first ten users come from. We already have evidence here: our notes cite editors asking for exactly this. Show up as an editor sharing a tool, never as a founder marketing one — that community punishes the second hard. First users
4 Adobe Exchange Official, appears inside Premiere, good for legitimacy. Slower review and less traffic than you would expect. Worth doing, not worth waiting for. Later

Selling direct from our own site is viable but means building checkout and licensing ourselves. Skip it initially.

Feedback Loop

Trading trial credit for a conversation

The FastLane tactic — a prominent button offering extended trial in exchange for booking a call — is a good one, and worth building. Two honest qualifications about when.

It presupposes a trial system we have not built. Time limits, licence keys, a way to grant an extension: none of that exists yet, and it is real engineering. Worth scoping before it is assumed.

At ten users the button is the slow path. Its value is converting strangers at volume. Right now we can simply message editors directly and ask — faster, warmer, and it produces better answers than an incentivised form. Build the button when the traffic exists to justify it.

One thing to watch when it does ship: people rewarded for feedback give generous feedback. Ask what they tried and abandoned, not whether they liked it. The most useful sentence a tester can produce is “I stopped using it when…”

Our Advantage

We can make the demo. Most plugin developers cannot.

Three years of Housing Day videos with 30K+ views and a film-festival win. That is precisely the skill this category rewards, and the thing we would otherwise pay someone to do badly.

The demo already exists, we just have to film it. Dom applies a blur by hand — seven actions. Then one keystroke. Then the moment that beats every competitor: press it on a different clip and it follows the selection. A Stream Deck macro cannot do that, and we can show it rather than claim it.

Do not outsource this. It is the highest-leverage asset we will make, and it is the one thing we are already better at than the people we are competing with.

Company

What we need before money changes hands

General orientation, not legal advice. The documents themselves are worth an hour of a real lawyer’s time — the cost is small next to what an unwritten assumption costs later.

Do we need an entity?

Strictly, no. Software can be sold by individuals. But there is a specific reason to consider one here: two people sharing profits can constitute a general partnership by default, without anyone signing anything — and in that arrangement each partner can be personally liable for obligations the other creates. An LLC separates the business from our personal finances.

It also makes “reinvest the profits” mean something. Without an entity, revenue is personal income to each of us that we then choose to spend on the product. With one, money can simply stay in the business. Worth asking an accountant how retained profits are taxed in our situation before assuming reinvestment is tax-free.

Selling through aescripts also lightens this considerably: the marketplace handles payment processing and much of the tax collection, which is a genuine argument for starting there rather than selling direct.

The founder agreement matters more than the entity

You are right that we need this now, while we agree about everything. The terms that cause the damage when missing:

IP assignment That the code belongs to the venture, not to whoever typed it. This is the most commonly skipped clause and the most destructive one to be missing.Critical
Split, and what it is a split of Profit share is not the same as ownership. Say which we mean, and how expenses come out first.
What happens if one of us stops The uncomfortable one, and the reason to write it while nobody is leaving. Vesting exists for exactly this.Do early
Who decides what Fine to be informal, but write down what needs both of us — pricing, selling, taking money in.
Expenses The developer account, certificates, a Windows machine. Who pays, and how it is recovered.

Liability, and why it is not zero

QuickKey edits people’s project files. The realistic bad day is not a lawsuit, it is a bug that damages an edit before a deadline. Two things follow.

We need an end-user licence with a warranty disclaimer and a liability cap. Standard, boilerplate, and it is what stands between a bad bug and a claim for someone’s lost billable work. Marketplaces supply usable terms, which is one more reason to launch through one.

And it raises the bar on testing. Every command should be undoable in Premiere’s own history — something we have already verified — and that property is worth protecting deliberately as features are added.

Biggest Gap

We have not decided about Windows

QuickKey is Mac-only, and a large share of Premiere editors are on Windows.

This is the most consequential thing neither of us has raised. Everything above — aescripts, reviewers, pricing — is being planned for a product that currently serves a fraction of its market.

The good news is that most of the work already ports. The panel is HTML and JavaScript and the Premiere-side code is ExtendScript; both run identically on Windows. Only the hotkey daemon is Mac-specific, because it uses macOS’s Carbon hotkey API.

A Windows version means rewriting that one component against Win32, plus a different installer and a Windows machine to test on. Call it a solid session of work and a device we do not own — not a rebuild.

Three ways to play it, and we should pick deliberately rather than by default:

  • Mac first, Windows after validation. Ship narrow, learn, port once we know people pay. Costs us reach and some reviewer interest.
  • Both at launch. Full market from day one, but delays everything by the port plus the testing we cannot do on our own machines.
  • Mac-only, permanently. Defensible for a niche premium tool, but it should be a decision we made, not one we drifted into.

Worth asking Dom what the split looks like among editors he actually knows. That answer is better evidence than any market figure we could look up.

Decide Together

Two calls to make before any of it

Pricing

The category norm is a one-time purchase, roughly $30–$100, rather than a subscription. Editors are subscription-fatigued from Adobe itself, and a subscription is only defensible with ongoing server costs — which we do not have.

Leaning: one-time, priced where an hour of saved editing pays for it.

What we owe people if Adobe breaks it

QuickKey depends on an undocumented Premiere layer that a point release could remove. That is our moat and our liability in one sentence. Decide now whether we promise version compatibility, and say it plainly on the sales page.

Being upfront is also a differentiator — buyers here have been burned by exactly this.

Two smaller things worth doing before the name is printed on anything

  • Check that “QuickKey” is free to use — a trademark search and a domain check. Cheap now, expensive after a launch video.
  • Read Adobe’s branding rules for third-party plugins. There are constraints on implying endorsement and on how “for Premiere Pro” may be worded.

And the honest caution: none of this is the most valuable unknown right now. That is still whether QuickKey holds up on ten machines that are not ours.
